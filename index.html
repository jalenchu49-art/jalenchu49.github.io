01 / ACADEMIC BIOGRAPHY

Understanding comes first.

Understanding human behavior through psychological theory and quantitative research.

Dr. Honglin Zhu is a psychologist focused on psychological research and data analysis. His research interests span social psychology, developmental psychology, child development, and human behavior. His academic work emphasizes empirical methods, statistical analysis, and scientific research design to explore how social environments, gender, and individual behavior influence cognition, social judgment, and interpersonal interaction.

Dr. Zhu has a background in psychology study and research at Brigham Young University–Idaho. His research experience includes a replication project on Roth and colleagues’ work concerning the moralization of effort in work and caregiving contexts. This research examines how perceived effort shapes moral evaluations, judgments of competence and fair compensation, and willingness to cooperate.

His research interests include operationalizing hypotheses, questionnaire design, variable measurement, data preparation, and interpretation of statistical results. He values replication as a way to assess the robustness and reproducibility of psychological findings. Relevant quantitative methods include between-group comparisons, analysis of variance, effect-size assessment, and Bayesian analysis; specific applications depend on the analyses actually completed.

In computing and data analysis, Dr. Zhu regards R as an important tool for quantitative psychological research. His interests include data cleaning, descriptive statistics, statistical inference, visualization, and reproducible analytical workflows. By combining statistical methods with psychological theory, he aims to strengthen research transparency, analytical reliability, and the interpretability of scientific conclusions.

Alongside social psychology, he is interested in developmental psychology and child development, including cognitive, emotional, and social development in children and adolescents, and the influences of family, school, and social environments on individual growth.

His long-term academic vision is to combine psychological theory, computational analysis, and rigorous empirical research to deepen understanding of human behavior and psychological development, and to support applications in education, social development, and university teaching.

Dr. Zhu earned his Ph.D. in Psychology from Brigham Young University in 2026 and his bachelor’s degree in 2025. His professional interests also include clinical depression and adolescent development.
